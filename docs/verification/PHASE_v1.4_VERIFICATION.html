Equilibrium Freeze/Thaw Extension + Resume
Verification report

Purpose. Preserve the 17 already completed cases and the four committed partial cases, while extending only phase-active cold states with an equilibrium liquid/ice partition and fusion latent heat. The original hmsl scientific-core files remain byte-identical.

1. What this update changes

The 1.4 execution adapter treats the existing water state as total condensable water expressed as liquid-water-equivalent volumetric content. When the source retention curve and temperature imply an equilibrium ice fraction, total water is partitioned algebraically into mobile liquid and immobile ice-equivalent water. Liquid-only moisture/salt transport is then solved with fusion latent heat in the energy balance. Transported Na2SO4-equivalent salt remains in the liquid phase, so freezing concentrates the dissolved-equivalent salt.

Whenever no equilibrium ice is present, the adapter calls the original derivative implementation verbatim. Existing completed cases are skipped rather than recalculated.

This is not a frost-damage model. It does not model ice-pressure damage, pore expansion mechanics, nucleation/supercooling hysteresis, a snow layer, or mixed-electrolyte activity. Salt freezing-point depression is not credited. The declared phase-extension temperature floor remains -20 C.

4. Numerical phase-event check

A short R14 phase-event test compared 200 cells against 400 cells and the base internal maximum step against a halved step. Halving the timestep produced zero difference for the checked metrics. The 200-vs-400 mesh comparison gave approximately 0.089% relative difference in final water, 0.000074% in final salt, and 0.041% in final mean temperature. Peak integrated ice-water amount differed by approximately 7.7%.

Interpretation: the transport/temperature quantities were strongly converged in this event test, but ice amount is more mesh-sensitive. The phase extension is suitable for continuing the moisture/heat/salt experiment and recording phase occurrence, but ice quantity should be treated as a diagnostic, not as a high-precision ice-mass or frost-damage prediction. The original 5% liquid-model QA is not silently re-labelled as a passed 5% phase-ice QA.

5. Scientific basis

The implementation follows the general thermodynamic-equilibrium treatment of freezing/thawing in porous building materials described by Matsumoto et al. (2001), while the liquid/ice vapour-pressure relationship uses Murphy & Koop (2005). IAPWS ice and supercooled-water formulations are retained as reference sources for the thermodynamic scope.

  • Matsumoto M., Hokoi S., Hatano M. (2001), Building and Environment 36:733-742. DOI: 10.1016/S0360-1323(00)00066-4.
  • Murphy D.M., Koop T. (2005), Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society 131:1539-1565. DOI: 10.1256/qj.04.94.
  • IAPWS, Revised Release on the Equation of State 2006 for H2O Ice Ih (2009 revision).
  • IAPWS, Guideline on Thermodynamic Properties of Supercooled Water.

6. What has not been certified here

The user's exact long-run R14, R17, R18 and R20 checkpoint states were not available in this build environment. Therefore this report does not claim that all four full remaining multi-decade runs have already completed. The installer/worker is designed to resume those exact checkpoints on the user's machine and preserve their committed work.

Native Windows execution was not available in the build environment. The Windows launcher/installer is supplied and uses the same backup-first safeguards as earlier add-ons.

7. Installation safety

  1. Pause/close all running Heritage Lab workers before installation.
  2. The installer verifies the original scientific-core hashes and the update payload.
  3. It creates and verifies a full project backup before replacing only the execution add-on/launcher.
  4. After launch, confirm the 17 completed cases are still COMPLETED and R14/R17/R18/R20 retain their nonzero saved hours before starting.

Bottom line: 1.4 is a real cold-phase equation extension, not a disabled guard or forced PASS. It is deliberately structured to preserve completed work, resume compatible checkpoints, conserve water/salt, and disclose the added phase assumptions.
